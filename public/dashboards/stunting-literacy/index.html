Childhood Stunting and Later Educational Outcomes

Childhood stunting* can have lasting consequences for health, learning, and educational attainment. This dashboard explores whether Philippine regions with higher under-five stunting prevalence in 2008 also tended to report lower senior-high completion in 2023 and lower functional literacy in 2024.

These outcomes are shaped by many interacting conditions—not stunting alone. Household income, parental education, school access and quality, conflict, displacement, healthcare, infrastructure, migration, language, and other social and economic factors can all influence regional results.

The results should be read as evidence of regional pattern persistence and as a starting point for further investigation—not as proof of a causal or longitudinal relationship.

*In this dashboard, childhood stunting refers to stunting among children under five years old (ages 0–59 months).

How to read this

This visualization compares regions—not individuals. It explores geographic patterns rather than cause and effect. Darker shades indicate more concerning values: higher stunting, lower completion, and lower literacy.

              Methodological interpretation

              How to interpret the comparison.

              01

              A regional—not individual—comparison

              Each point represents a region, not a person or group followed over time. A downward pattern means that regions with higher stunting in 2008 also tended to have lower educational outcomes later. It does not show that the children measured in 2008 experienced those outcomes individually.

              02

              Association is not causation

              Stunting and educational outcomes may share underlying influences, including poverty, food insecurity, parental education, health services, school access and quality, conflict, disasters, rural isolation, and infrastructure. The dashboard does not separate the independent effect of any one factor.

              03

              Different surveys and populations

              The indicators come from separate surveys and administrative sources. Stunting covers children under five in 2008; senior-high completion is a regional education-system measure for 2023; functional literacy covers people ages 10–64 in 2024. Regional boundaries also changed during this period, particularly with the creation of the Negros Island Region.

              The comparison is a starting point for further investigation—not proof of a causal or longitudinal relationship.
